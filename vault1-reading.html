The Little Mouse and the Lion
Part 2 — The Little Mouse and the Lion

Once a little mouse entered the den of a lion. The lion became very angry to see him there.

He roared in a thundering voice and said, “How dare you intrude my den just like that? I will kill you!”

Nowhere to run, the poor little mouse began trembling with fear. He said, “Please do not kill me. I request you kindly to spare my life. Though, I am too small and weak to do anything for you. But, still I promise that I shall remember your kindness throughout my life and shall feel obliged, if I could do anything for you at any point of time.”

The lion looked at his small size and said with a smile, “You are too small and weak to do anything for a powerful creature like me. But do not worry. I am going to spare your life. But, remember not to enter my den in the future. You can go now.”

“Thank you very much, sir,” said the little mouse still trembling with fear. He left, but he was indeed very grateful to the lion.

One day, a hunter set a net with skilful maneuver to trap the lion and hid himself in a tree.

Meanwhile, the lion was wandering in search of prey. He did not notice the contrivance and stepped into it. And before he could understand anything, he was trapped.

This was so sudden that the lion was taken aback. He roared loudly and took up the cudgels. But all in vain. The net proved to be beyond his physical power.

The little mouse heard him roaring and immediately understood that the lion was in great trouble. He came out of his hole and saw the lion trapped.

The little mouse went near him, expressed his sympathies and said, “Have patience. I shall nibble at the net and free you within no time.”

“Little mouse,” said the lion, “Do you really think you shall be able to free me from this trap? I am at least a thousand times stronger than you and despite all my efforts have not been able to free myself and you? Being so small a creature, say that you shall free me. Funny! Isn’t it?”

“Sir, just wait and see,” said the little mouse.

And really, within a very short time the little mouse nibbled at the net in hundreds of places. Soon, the lion was freed from the net.

Now it was the lion’s turn. He was full of gratitude. He gave him hearty thanks. And the little mouse was happy that at last he could do something for the lion.

Adapted from English for Students, 2020. Source: MUET Session 3 2022 Reading paper, p. 4.

Visit to the Lotus Temple
Part 3 — A Boy’s Visit to the Lotus Temple

A visit to a historical place that you’ve never been to is always an exciting experience. It is a fascinating adventure. I had one such experience during the last summer vacation, when I visited Delhi.

With my family, I reached Delhi by train. After some rest, we took a tour to Delhi. Delhi is a historical city. I found it unique because it has been called by different names in different periods of time. On one hand, there are the Red Fort, the Jama Masjid, the Jantar Mantar, the Qutub Minar, the India Gate, which narrate the glorious past of India; while the Rashtrapati Bhawan, the Lotus Temple, Parliament, to name a few, are the wonders of modern times.

We visited the Lotus Temple. The beautiful lotus-shaped temple was opened in the year 1986, and since then has invited many local and foreign visitors to its premises. Well known for its flowerlike shape, it has become one of the biggest attractions in Delhi. Although it is built for Bahai followers, the Lotus Temple is open to all, regardless of faith or any other qualification.

The entry to the temple is through a gate that leads to a pathway with landscaped gardens on both sides. Because of its holiness, cleanliness is a must. So, we had to take off our shoes and leave them on the shoe racks. From there, we stood in a queue until we could enter the temple.

Once inside, it was complete silence and peace took over. We saw people of different religions sitting and praying. The hall inside has nine doors and a capacity to seat 2500 people at one time. We spent quite some time there and then came out.

By noon, we had completed the tour of the Lotus Temple and returned. I enjoyed my trip to the Lotus Temple very much. It was really an unforgettable experience apart from being intellectually rewarding, gave us a glimpse of our country’s proud history.

Adapted from All Essay, 2016. Source: MUET Session 3 2022 Reading paper, p. 6.

Environmental Problems
Part 4 — Environmental Problems

Text 1 · The Greenhouse Effect

The greenhouse effect is a process that occurs when gases in Earth’s atmosphere trap the sun’s heat. This process makes Earth much warmer than it would be without an atmosphere. The greenhouse effect is one of the things that makes Earth a comfortable place to live.

As you might expect from the name, the greenhouse effect works like a greenhouse. A greenhouse has glass walls and roof that trap heat, keeping plants warm even in winter. Greenhouse gases such as carbon dioxide likewise trap heat released from Earth’s surface at night. This keeps Earth at a warm average of 58 degrees Fahrenheit (14 degrees Celsius).

Human activities are changing the natural greenhouse effect. Burning fossil fuels puts more carbon dioxide into the atmosphere, trapping more heat. Plants can help balance this effect by taking in carbon dioxide and giving off oxygen. Oceans also absorb carbon dioxide, but this makes the water more acidic and can harm shellfish and coral. Warmer waters contribute to coral bleaching.

Adapted from NASA Climate Kids, 2020.

Text 2 · Global Warming

Natural disasters, terrorist attacks and nuclear accidents can affect many people, but their impacts are usually confined to a region or continent. Global warming is different: a gradual rise in Earth’s temperature that could transform the entire planet. It could make climate more erratic, drive species to extinction and make life harder, especially in developing countries.

Imagine a timber shack in Alaska with a large log fire. If the fire supplies heat at the same rate as the shack loses it, its temperature stays stable. Make the fire too big and the shack grows hotter even after you stop adding wood. Global warming works similarly: Earth warms slightly year by year. Since 1900 the planet has warmed by about 0.8 degrees Celsius. By the end of the 21st century, an increase of around 2 to 5 degrees Celsius is likely. Five degrees is roughly the difference between today’s world and the last Ice Age; that earlier change took about 5000 years, whereas modern warming could occur within a century. Once the planet starts warming, slowing the process is difficult.

Adapted from Explain That Stuff, 2022. Source: MUET Session 3 2022 Reading paper, pp. 8–10.

Running in the Family
Part 6 — Running in the Family

The early twenties had been a busy and expensive time for my grandparents. They spent most of the year in Colombo and during the hot months of April and May moved to Nuwara Eliya. In various family journals, there are references made to the time spent “upcountry” away from the lowland heat. Cars would leave Colombo and perform the tiring five-hour journey, the radiators steaming as they wound their way up into the mountains. Books and sweaters and golf clubs and rifles were packed into trunks, children were taken out of school, dogs were bathed and made ready for the drive.

Nuwara Eliya was a different world. One did not sweat there and only those who had asthma tried to avoid these vacations. At an elevation of 6000 feet, the families could look forward to constant parties, horse racing, the All Ceylon Tennis Tournament, and serious golf. Although the best Sinhalese tennis players competed upcountry, they would move back to Colombo if they had to play champions from other nations as the excessive heat could be guaranteed to destroy the visitors. And so, while monsoon and heat moved into deserted Colombo homes, it was to Nuwara Eliya that my grandparents and their circle of friends would go. They danced in large living rooms to the music of a Bijou-Moutrie piano, while the log fires crackled in every room, or on quiet evenings read books on the moonlit porch, slicing open the pages as they progressed through a novel.

The gardens were full of cypress, rhododendrons, foxgloves, arum lilies, and sweet peas; and people like the Van Langenbergs, the Vernon Dickmans, the Henry de Mels and the Philip Ondaatjes were there. There were casual tragedies such as Lucas Cantley’s wife, Jessica, almost died after being shot by an unknown assailant while playing croquet with my grandfather. They found 113 pellets in her. And poor Wilfred Batholomeusz who had large teeth, kicked the bucket while out hunting when one of his companions mistook him for a wild boar. Most of the men belonged to the CLI reserves and usually borrowed guns when going on vacation.

It was in Nuwara Eliya that Dick de Vos danced with his wife Etta, who fell flat on the floor; she had not danced for years. He picked her up, deposited her on a cane chair, came over to Rex Daniels and said, “Now you know why I gave up dancing and took to drink.” Each morning the men departed for the club to play a game of billiards. They would arrive around eleven in buggy carts pulled by bulls and play until the afternoon rest hours while the punkah, the large cloth fan, floated and waved above them and the twenty or so bulls snorted in a circle around the clubhouse. Major Robinson, who ran the prison, would officiate at the tournaments.

During the month of May, the circus came to Nuwara Eliya. Once, when the circus lights failed, Major Robinson drove the fire engine into the tent and focused the headlights on the trapeze artist, who had no intention of continuing and sat there straddling his trapeze. At one of these touring circuses my Aunt Christie, then only twenty-five, stood up and volunteered to have an apple shot off her head by a total stranger in the circus profession. That night, T. W. Roberts was bitten in the leg by a dog while he danced with her. Later the dog was discovered to be rabid, but as T. W. Roberts had left for England, nobody bothered to tell him. Most assume he survived. They were all there, Piggford of the police, Paynter the planter, the Finnellis who were Baptist missionaries — “she being an artist and a very good tap dancer”.

This was Nuwara Eliya in the twenties and thirties. Everyone was vaguely related and had Sinhalese, Tamil, Dutch, British, and Burgher blood in them going back many generations. There was a large social gap between this circle and the Europeans and English who were never part of the Ceylonese community. The English were seen as transients and were quite separate from those who had intermarried and who lived here permanently. My father always claimed to be a Ceylon Tamil, though that was probably more valid about three centuries earlier. Emil Daniels summed up the situation for most of them when he was asked by one of the British governors what his nationality was — “God alone knows, your excellency.”

Philip Ondaatje was supposed to have the greatest collection of wine glasses in the Orient — my other grandfather, Willy Gratiaen, dreamt of snakes. Both my grandmothers lived cautiously, at least until their husbands died. Then they blossomed, especially Lalla who managed to persuade all those she met into chaos. It was Lalla who told us that the twenties were so whimsical, so busy — that we were always tired.

Adapted from Michael Ondaatje’s Running in the Family, 1982. Source: MUET Session 3 2022 Reading paper, pp. 14–15.

IVF Treatment in Nigeria
Part 7 — A Treatment to Get Pregnant

Over 180 million couples suffer from infertility worldwide, a majority of whom are in developing countries, according to the World Health Organization (WHO). The main symptom of infertility is that the woman cannot get pregnant but the problem could lie with either the woman or the man. The WHO estimates that infertility affects up to 10.5% of couples of reproductive age group globally and 20% of couples of the same group in Nigeria. More than eight million babies have been born as a result of assisted pregnancies, according to data from the WHO.

Assisted pregnancy means that medical techniques are used to bring about the conception and birth of a child. An example of assisted pregnancy is in vitro fertilization (IVF). In IVF, mature eggs are collected from the ovaries of a woman and fertilised with sperms of a man in a lab. The fertilised embryo is then put into the womb of the woman. While assisted pregnancy gives infertile couples the chance to have a child, a recent study has shown that the children born this way are more likely to develop high blood pressure in their teens. In addition, because of the lack of regulation, the dishonest practices in the IVF industry endanger not only childless couples, but the country in general. Hospitals in Nigeria charge about 1.5 to 3.5 million naira (about USD3000 to USD7000) for IVF treatment but “quacks” (incompetent doctors) charge as low as NGN350 000.

Available statistics shows that there are about 12 million infertile persons in Nigeria, which is almost 10% of the total population. On the rising cases of infertility, Professor of Obstetrics and Gynecology, University of Tokyo, Japan, Yutaka Osuga, explained to The Guardian that age is one of the major causes leading to rise in infertility. He said, “most couples seek IVF treatment at an older age, especially when they are 40 years old. So the success rate of the IVF procedure is low as the woman does not produce enough eggs.” The impact of this is particularly prominent in cultures where there is strong emphasis on childbirth, like Nigeria and other African countries, leading to social, psychological, and financial challenges.

The IVF procedures have helped couples to deal with the high rate of infertility, but many factors slow down the growth of the IVF treatment market in Africa. Some of these factors are poor income, high cost, complications associated with IVF treatment, low awareness regarding IVF, inadequacy of healthcare services, and lack of trained healthcare professionals. The estimated number of IVF clinics in Nigeria is about 70, while the total number in Africa is near 160. This is a small number compared to the number of clinics in Chicago city alone, which is over 80. According to the Medical Director of Medical Art Centre (MART Clinics), Professor Oladapo Ashiru, these statistics show that Nigeria is now recognised worldwide as having expertise in assisted pregnancy because 95% of IVF cases are treated in Nigeria by Nigerians.

Recently, the MART Clinics raised alarm that there have been cases of quacks performing IVF treatment. The situation has grown worse in recent years with unlicensed doctors boldly operating in public and private hospitals to take advantage of the demand. According to a 2014 survey in Nigeria, over half of the population had received treatment from quacks. He said, “if this becomes a public knowledge, Nigerians would lose faith in the centres available in the country and start getting IVF treatment in other countries, which could hurt the industry.”

Professor Oladapo Ashiru further stressed that the specialists have a very big task in Nigeria and need to address it. He said, “just as the specialists cannot overcharge, they cannot also lower the price, and the price for different types of procedures must be clearly stated.” At the same time, governmental authorities need to provide an acceptable code of practice, with regular revision of the practice code in line with the latest research and societal norms to meet patients’ expectations. He added that the process would be completed in the next few months. A list of licensed centres would be published to guide patients to the right centre for IVF treatment. “Ethics is not putting a policy document out there, it is the foundation of our practice and it is important to protect the industry from being ruined by quacks,” he noted.

Adapted from The Guardian, 2018. Source: MUET Session 3 2022 Reading paper, pp. 17–18.
